### ABSTRACT

Occupancy-based pattern mining has been researched and developed by researchers as an interesting field of data mining. It discovers high occupancy patterns that have a high proportion in their transactions. The results of the occupancy-based approach are useful for deriving hidden knowledge because the patterns are more valuable compared to traditional frequent patterns. Recently, an incremental method for high occupancy patterns has been suggested to handle transactions inserted in real-time. However, it often generates results that are not realistic. Even if it is suitable for processing dynamic databases, it is not appropriate to make intelligent decisions because the values of old and new transactions are deemed identical. In this paper, we propose an efficient approach for mining high occupancy patterns, considering the flow of time, with the damped window model. By assigning greater importance to recent data, our method allows for the discovery of more meaningful results following the latest trends. The patterns generated by the novel approach encourage users to intelligently discover occupancy-driven patterns over time-sensitive databases. In addition, the designed method relies on a list-based data structure that increases efficiency without generating candidates, and it utilizes a new pruning strategy to greatly reduce the search space. Extensive experiments using both real and synthetic datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach shows better performance in accordance with varying thresholds and exhibits high efficiency consistently, regardless of the increase in the number of transactions and discrete items.

## 1. Introduction

Pattern mining is one branch of the data mining technique (Baek and Doleck, 2022). The field of pattern mining has been actively studied because the technique, such as association rule mining (Agrawal and Srikant, 1994; Chen et al., 2022), can accelerate data-driven decisions effectively. As a representative pattern mining technique, frequent pattern mining (Ahmed and sNath, 2021; Bicer et al., 2021) is applied to real-world domains such as web click data analysis or service (Huynh et al., 2020), internet of things (Ismail et al., 2018), social network (Choi and Park, 2019), and network service (Santos and Mello, 2022). For this reason, different variations have emerged in diverse fields (Fournier-Viger et al., 2022; Liang et al., 2020; Cai et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2014; Karthik et al., 2020).

However, frequency-based processing frameworks occasionally cannot handle all the needs of real-life users effectively. For example, a real-life market database may encompass factors in addition to support, such as item-specific price, profit, and importance. However, the frequency-driven mining approach does not take into account these factors, and thus it cannot obtain the desired high-quality results, such as profitable itemsets. Thus, the measure that considers only the support of patterns can harm intelligent and automated systems. Amid the backdrop, a variety of pattern mining techniques using valuable measures have emerged. Utility-driven pattern mining (Gan et al., 2021; Nguyen et al., 2021; Nguyen et al., 2020) extracts patterns with high utility from a non-binary database and the importance of items. Since then, advanced pattern mining techniques considering ambiguous factors, such as fuzzy theory for human reasoning (Ganesan and Shankar, 2022; Lin et al., 2021) and noise/error (Baek, Yun, Kim, Kim, Vo, Truong and Deng, 2021a), have appeared.

Meanwhile, a new approach (Tang et al., 2012), named high occupancy pattern mining, was proposed. High occupancy pattern mining considers the proportion of patterns in the transactions where the patterns exist. In a transactional database, the occupancy of a pattern means the proportion that the pattern occupies in a transaction, which is calculated by dividing the length of the pattern by that of the transaction. It is noted that this approach finds dominant and high ratio patterns in transactions instead of patterns discovered by the aforementioned approaches relying on utility or support. Based on this advantageous aspect, the technique is utilized in real-world applications, such as recommendation systems (Adhikari, 2020). In the scenario of a retail shop, the technique can be utilized for automated recommendation services by considering items that are frequently sold as well as items that consume large proportions in purchase transactions. In this way, results encompass much of users' purchase motivations. Expanding on the subject of recommender systems, Tang et al. (2012) also proposed printout area recommendations for websites and investment portfolio recommendations as their motivating applications. Since then, algorithms (Deng, 2020; Datta et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2015) for extracting patterns with high occupancy have been proposed. However, previous high occupancy pattern mining studies perform mining operations only on already accumulated static data, so they still have a problem processing real-world data that occurs in real-time. As a suitable example, assume an IoT service such as a body sensor (Gochoo et al., 2021) that reflects the activity of humans (Liu et al., 2019). According to human activities, the body sensor accumulates information, such as the user's behaviors or movements. Because the information is accumulated in real-time, the sensor should be needed to quickly process and analyze it. However, methods that effectively process only static data are inefficient for services that interact with humans. Hence, incremental approaches (Cheng et al., 2021; Kang et al., 2020; Yun et al., 2021) scan only the inserted transactions and handle incremental databases with efficient methods. This is appropriate for devices and services in real-world dynamic situations. In the field of occupancy-based pattern mining, several methods optimized for incremental databases have been developed, such as HOMI (Kim et al., 2022) and HUOMI (Ryu et al., 2022).

However, they are still unable to handle stream data effectively. This is because they do not take into account the fact that interest in recent data may be higher. In addition, the importance of older data tends to decrease as data accumulates. To deal with this problem, an effective model called the damped window model (Baek et al., 2020; Kim and Yun, 2017) has emerged. This model uses a method for reducing the importance of older transactions by multiplying a time-decaying factor by the measure of patterns. By utilizing the decaying factor, the damped window-based pattern mining can provide users with refined patterns reflecting the latest trends compared to other incremental methods under a time-sensitive environment. For this reason, several pattern mining studies, based on the damped window model, have been actively conducted in order to respond to users' interest in the data stream (Kim et al., 2021; Kim et al., 2021; Nam et al., 2020). One practical application of our research is automatic recommendation services for time-sensitive environments, such as in retail business. The service can provide users with more relevant results by assigning greater importance to more recent data in order to consider the latest trends or patterns. For example, even if a product occupies a large portion of its transactions in a retail database, it may not be considered interesting in damped window model environments if it is irrelevant within recent transactions. As a result, the product is not recommended to users as it does not align with the latest trends. The incremental high occupancy pattern mining approach, on the other hand, treats all transactions as having the same importance, regardless of their arrival times.

Motivated by the above issues and methods, in this paper, we propose an approach and algorithm that mines high occupancy patterns in data streams while utilizing a newly developed measure that considers both the occupancy measure and the transaction arrival time. Furthermore, the proposed method presents a new upper bound to ensure an efficient and rational pattern exploration range while effectively managing the costs associated with acquisitiveness and decaying factor calculations during the mining process. To the best of our knowledge, our paper is the first to address time-sensitive stream databases to discover high occupancy patterns. This paper has the following three contributions and novelties. 1) A novel and efficient DHOPM algorithm is suggested in order to deal with high occupancy patterns that consider the different importance of transactions according to their arrival times, using the developed measure called damped occupancy (DO). The framework of the time-decaying model, or damped window model, effectively discovers recent patterns, and no previous occupancy pattern mining study covered this approach. 2) A list-based data structure is proposed to effectively store and maintain the values in consideration of the time-decaying factor. Based on this data structure, an efficient upper bound is also designed to reduce the computational costs during the mining process compared to existing methods. With this feature, DHOPM is able to effectively process damped high occupancy patterns without any information loss. 3) Performance tests between DHOPM and the cutting-edge algorithms were conducted with various settings on different types of real and synthetic datasets. Experimental results show that DHOPM performs better than state-of-the-art occupancy pattern mining algorithms.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the studies related to the proposed algorithm to understand incremental pattern mining and occupancy-based pattern mining. In Section 3, the proposed algorithm is explained in detail with examples and figures. In Section 4, we compare the proposed algorithm with other cutting-edge algorithms in various datasets and environments. The limitations of the proposed method and future work are discussed in Section 5. Finally, we will conclude this paper in Section 6.

## 2. Related work

### 2.1. High occupancy pattern mining

Traditional support-based pattern mining is interested in patterns that occur frequently in a given database. The patterns extracted by this approach are valuable, but it is difficult to discover quality patterns from the real world and apply them to a variety of applications since its main measure is support. Meanwhile, Tang et al. (2012) proposed a concept called average occupancy, which is the proportion of a pattern in the transactions in which the pattern occurred. The algorithm, DOFIA, extracts such patterns whose occupancy and support are no less than the given thresholds. Then Zhang et al. (2015) suggested a method combining sequence data and the occupancy concept, which accompanied the DOFRA algorithm. The previous mining approaches, however, had the problem of requiring knowledge from the user due to the two thresholds. Deng (2020) designed a new kind of mining method based on occupancy. The author introduced an interestingness measure by integrating support and occupancy. The proposed HEP algorithm is not only a level-wise method but also employs an occupancy-list structure. In addition, an upper bound was used to achieve high efficiency. As opposed to the above methods, approaches that harmonize utility and occupancy have emerged in order to further reflect the reality of real-world items. The utility occupancy measure was first proposed in OCEAN (Shen et al., 2016). Despite the usefulness of utility occupancy, it suffered from incompleteness in mining and performance degradation. To address these drawbacks, the HUOPM algorithm (Gan et al., 2020) has been studied. HUOPM employs two data structures called UO-list and FU-table. Furthermore, several pruning strategies that are not used in OCEAN are also used in HUOPM. An approach for finding high utility occupancy patterns in databases where each item has existence probability was proposed in UHUOPM (Chen et al., 2021). As another application of occupancy, HOIMTO (Datta et al., 2022) utilizing the occupancy of transactions has been suggested. Occupancy-driven pattern mining approaches have been steadily proposed and developed, but nevertheless have limitations in the real-time processing of additional transactions in the real world.

### 2.2. Pattern mining in incremental environments

The aforementioned methods are not possible to mine results in an incremental environment because there is a limitation of scratching previously accumulated data. Therefore, researchers have conducted advanced studies on handling stream environments in various pattern mining fields. MTS-FPM (Karaca et al., 2022) is a tree-based algorithm to mine frequent patterns from multivariate time series with only one scan, which required the counting of the supports of 1-length itemsets. PCPT-FCI (Liu et al., 2022) is an efficient algorithm over data streams where transaction deletion occurs, and it includes a prefix tree, called PCPT, to facilitate tree search quickly. FIUFP-Growth (Thurachon and Kreesuradej, 2021) is a frequent pattern mining method maintaining the previously discovered frequent patterns and their support information. It employs an updated tree structure changed by incremental databases. Meanwhile, there are incremental mining techniques considering the utility measure, not support. IIHUM (Yun, Nam, Lee and Yoon, 2019a) is a single scan-based utility mining algorithm. It scans an incremental database and stores utility information in its own indexed list structure, which is a more efficient data structure for the mining process than tree and list structures. Also, a list-based PIHUPM algorithm (Kim et al., 2023), which includes the pre-large concept, was proposed to reduce the number of scans for database updates due to transaction additions. SOHUPDS (Jaysawal and Huang, 2020) based on the projected database scheme (Nguyen et al., 2019) leverages a sliding window technique and a list structure in order to find high utility patterns over data streams. The method achieves the goal of a single-pass approach, addressing incremental problems by updating the current window. FCHM-Stream (Li et al., 2023) also employs the sliding window-based principle to process incremental streams, but it discovers closed high utility patterns. It incorporates list structures that include a batch division skill and a maintenance technique. In addition, there are a high average-utility pattern mining methods (Truong, Duong, Le, Fournier-Viger & Yun, 2022a, 2022b), which measure the average utility by considering the length of patterns. They also effectively respond to the update of the database due to the data insertion. Although the above methods are capable of handling incremental databases, they do not utilize the occupancy factor, which provides insightful information on data.

### 2.3. Occupancy pattern mining in incremental environments

As an incremental high occupancy pattern mining method, Kim et al. (2022) proposed a list-based method to extract high occupancy patterns in an incremental environment. The HOMI algorithm in this method employs HOMI-list, a list-based data structure, to avoid generating and checking candidate patterns differently from tree-based ones (Thurachon and Kreesuradej, 2021). HOMI conducts an incremental updating process whenever incremental data occurs, and thus HOMI realizes one scan-based mining operation in an incremental situation. There are two core differences between HOMI and the previous list-based algorithm, HEP (Deng, 2020). Firstly, HEP lacks methods for handling incremental data, whereas HOMI has single scan-based methods, which include scan and reconstruction processes. Secondly, HOMI conducts pattern expansion in a DFS fashion including 2-way comparison to find common transaction TIDs, whereas HEP adopts a naïve level-wise approach. For this reason, HOMI showed better performance than HEP in terms of runtime and memory usage. Recently, there has been an incremental high-utility occupancy mining approach that includes the HUOMI algorithm (Ryu et al., 2022). It performs better in an incremental environment than other existing utility occupancy-driven ones (Gan et al., 2020; Shen et al., 2016). Meanwhile, approaches for effectively handling data streams have also been raised (Yun, Lee and Yoon, 2019b), which adopted models that address time-sensitive features of the data streams. Among these, the damped window model relatively increases the importance of recent transactions while lowering the importance of old ones. Various damped window-based pattern mining algorithms have emerged, which include damped high utility pattern mining (Baek, Yun, Kim, Nam, Kim, Lin, Vo and Pedrycz, 2021b) and damped high average-utility pattern mining (Kim, J. et al., 2021; Yun et al., 2018). As a result, they can obtain more useful patterns by preventing the extraction of absolute knowledge over time. While there are many damped window-based pattern mining methods, there is no approach for damped window-based occupancy-driven pattern mining for high-quality decision-making.

## 3. List-based damped high occupancy pattern mining

### 3.1. Preliminaries

In this section, the essential definitions based on existing approaches (Deng, 2020; Kim et al., 2022), as well as novel definitions proposed in this paper, are introduced for the approach to extract damped high utility patterns. Furthermore, practical examples are provided based on the example database in Table 1 for a clear understanding of readers. In Table 1, incremental databases are shown, and they are expressed as DB0, DB1, and DB2. Let the set of unique items in the entire database be I = {i1, i2, …, in}. Each incremental database has multiple transactions that include discrete items in I. In addition, each transaction (Td ⊆ I) includes TID and several items, ik ∈ Td. Suppose that there is a pattern X in a database DB. The support of X refers to the number of transactions that contain X in DB and is denoted as Sup(X).

**Definition 1.** (Occupancy of a pattern). Assume that there is a pattern X, and X exists in a transaction Td. In Td, the occupancy of X means the ratio of the length of X to the length of Td, and is represented by O(X,Td). In a database DB, the occupancy of X can be calculated by the summation of O(X, Td), where Td ∈ DB, and it is expressed as O(X). Below are the two formulas of the occupancy of X.

O(X, Td) = |X| / |Td| (1)

When a user provides a threshold percentage ∂, the minimum support threshold in a database DB is calculated by multiplying ∂ and the cardinality of DB. In other words, the minimum support threshold of DB can be calculated as minSup = ∂ × |DB|. It is noted that the range of ∂ is limited to between 0 and 1. If the occupancy of pattern X is greater than or equal to minSup, it is considered a high occupancy pattern. It means that X occupies a large portion of transactions for the entire database. i.e., X is a high occupancy pattern if minSup ≤ O(X).

**Definition 2.** (Upper Bound of Occupancy, UBO). Since occupancy does not follow the anti-monotone property, an upper bound is required to eliminate unpromising patterns. UBO (Upper Bound of Occupancy) is used as an overestimation of a pattern's occupancy. Let T be a set of transactions where the transactions have u different lengths. The set of lengths of these transactions in ascending order is represented by L = {l1, l2, …, lx, …, lu}. Each element has its own value, nx, which is the number of transactions whose length is lx. When there is a pattern X (X ⊆ Tk ∈ T), the upper bound of occupancy of X is defined as follows.

UBO(X, x) = ∑ᵢ₌ₓᵘ nᵢ × lx/li (3)

UBO(X) = max₁≤x≤u {UBO(X, x)} (4)

In the paper (Deng, 2020) suggesting UBO, O(X') ≤ UBO(X) ≤ Sup(X) is proved, where X' indicates a superset of X. Therefore, it is possible to remove the super patterns of X in advance by adopting UBO(X), which is tighter than support. This is why UBO is used to satisfy the anti-monotone property. A pattern X is a high upper bound occupancy pattern if UBO(X) is greater than or equal to minSup. Let X' be a super pattern of a pattern X. In this case, the inequality, O(X') ≤ UBO(X), is established. Accordingly, if X is not a high upper bound occupancy pattern, all super patterns of X cannot be a high occupancy pattern.

**Definition 3.** (Decaying factor constant, f). Suppose there is a stream database DB = {T1,T2,…,Td,…}. The value f (0 < f < 1) to consider the importance of transactions over time is called the decaying factor constant and is set to a value defined by the user.

**Definition 4.** (Damped Occupancy of a pattern). The damped occupancy of a pattern X can consider temporal properties of patterns, which the existing O(X) cannot. Unlike before, this value is weighted by the time each transaction occurs, making it easier to identify the latest trends, considering the latest data. Below are formulas for the damped occupancy of a pattern, DO(X), where TL indicates the TID of the latest inserted transaction.

DO(X, Td) = O(X, Td) × f^(TL−Td) (5)

DO(X) = ∑ DO(X, Td), 𝑋⊆∀𝑇𝑑∈𝐷𝐵 (6)

**Definition 5.** (Damped high occupancy pattern). A pattern X is a damped high occupancy pattern if DO(X) is no less than minSup. This means that X occupies a high percentage of items in transactions involving itself. When DOPs is defined as a set of damped high occupancy patterns, it is obtained as DOPs = {X|DO(X) ≥ minSup}.

### 3.2. Overall process of DHOPM

The proposed method, DHOPM, performs several procedures to yield damped high occupancy patterns. These procedures rely on both a single-pass framework, which can handle incremental databases, and a list-based data structure, DHO-List. An overview of the process of mining high occupancy patterns by considering time-sensitive features in an incremental circumstance is shown in Fig. 1. As shown in this figure, DHOPM follows three procedures: 1) Construct or update the global DHO-List; 2) Reconstruct the global DHO-List for mining; 3) Mine damped high occupancy patterns through a DFS-based pattern expansion procedure. First, the initial global DHO-List for patterns with length 1 is constructed and maintained in the main memory after scanning a given database just once. It includes two types of tasks. One is to create the global DHO-List for the first time, and the other is to update the existing DHO-List. Then, when there is a mining request, the DHO-List nodes are sorted in support ascending order, using the supports of items calculated in the construction stage. If the sorting operation is complete, the damped occupancy values of the nodes are calculated using the decaying factor constant. The damped occupancy values of entries in a node are cumulated in a specific element, called DO, on each DHO-List node. It is noted that the decaying factor must be applied to the occupancy value in order to obtain damped occupancy. Finally, DHOPM performs the mining procedure so as to identify the validity of patterns with lengths greater than one. It adopts a DFS-based pattern-growth approach to produce long patterns, which is a recursive way of selecting a common prefix and combining patterns of the same length. In addition, this process involves comparisons with the threshold in order to distinguish result patterns, as well as strategies for efficient search space pruning using a new upper bound. As a result of the mining procedure, all damped high occupancy patterns are provided to the user. The following sections provide a detailed description of each procedure.

### 3.3. Constructing the global DHO-list with one-scan

This section describes the structure of DHO-List, the data structure used in DHOPM, and the construction process of the global DHO-List. DHOPM constructs the list structure by scanning the database once for dynamic environments. In dynamic environments, patterns that were not damped high occupancy patterns can become valid patterns through the addition of incremental databases. Therefore, even if mining is terminated for a single request, the global DHO-List after processing the database up to that point must be preserved. In the reconstructing process, which is explained in the next section, all established nodes in the global DHO-List are sorted in the support ascending order of items. For this reason, the support values of items are also maintained as part of the global list. A DHO-List consists of nodes corresponding to a particular item or pattern, and each node consists of a name, DO, and a set of entries. The name field stores the last item of the pattern, excluding the common prefix. However, unlike patterns, for items that do not have a prefix, the item itself is stored in the field. The DO field stores the sum of damped occupancy values for all entries of the item or pattern, and it is initialized to 0 in the construction process. Finally, the entry set consists of entries with a transaction identifier and length information of the transactions where the pattern occurred, each represented by TID and TLen.

The global DHO-List is constructed by scanning the original database once. Each transaction in the database is processed in order. Let the current scanned transaction be Tk. When scanning Tk, each item in Tk is processed sequentially. If there is no DHO-List node corresponding to the item in the global DHO-List, the node is newly created and inserted into the global DHO-List. The DO of the created list is initialized to 0, and an entry consisting of <k, |Tk|> is created and inserted into the entry set. In contrast, if the node exists, a new entry with the same values is inserted into the entry set of the corresponding node. Finally, the item's support increases by 1. This procedure is repeated for all items and transactions in the original database, after which the construction of the global DHO-List is complete.

The length of T1 is four, and it consists of items A, C, D, and E. Item A is processed first. There is no DHO-List node corresponding to A in the global DHO-List. Therefore, a new node is created and the item name and DO fields are set to A and 0, respectively. Then, an entry <1, 4> is inserted into the entry set of the node. Next, item C is processed. Since the node corresponding to C also does not exist in the global DHO-List, a new node is generated and inserted into the global DHO-List. The name of the item, C, is configured, DO is initialized to 0, and entry <1, 4> is inserted. DHO-List nodes corresponding to items D and E are created and inserted in the same manner. Lastly, the support values of all items in T1 increase by 1. Fig. 2 (B) indicates the global DHO-List after scanning T1 and T2. T2 is composed of items A, E, and F. ### 3.5. Updating the global DHO-list with incremental databases

When an additional database is inserted in a dynamic environment, DHOPM performs its updating procedure to reflect new information into the global DHO-List. For efficiency, DHOPM only scans the additional database once and does not scan the already processed databases. Note that when the updating process starts, the previously calculated DO values are initialized to zero, not retained. This is because the decaying factors that were previously applied lose their effect when additional transactions are inserted. When a new transaction Tk is scanned, items in Tk are processed sequentially. Processing scanned items during the updating procedure works in the same manner as in the construction procedure explained in Section 3.3. In other words, if a node corresponding to the scanned item does not exist in the global DHO-List, a new node is created. The DO of the new node is initialized to 0, and an entry of <k, |Tk|> is inserted. Conversely, if the corresponding node already exists, a new entry is inserted into the node without creating a new one. After this procedure is repeated for all items and transactions in the incremental database, the updating of the global DHO-List is complete.

### 3.6. Mining high occupancy patterns with the damped window model

After the reconstructing procedure is complete, DHOPM proceeds with the mining procedure. DHOPM's mining procedure operates using a DFS pattern expansion method with a prefix. This is because the DFS method with the prefix is runtime and memory efficient. First, the algorithm traverses all sorted DHO-List nodes in order and selects an item as a prefix. However, the high occupancy pattern does not satisfy the anti-monotone property. Therefore, it is necessary to calculate an overestimated occupancy value to inspect the expandability of a pattern. To solve this, HEP (Deng, 2020) proposed UBO, or upper bound occupancy. While this overestimated value satisfies the anti-monotone property, it does not regard the arrival time of transactions. Therefore, we propose a new upper bound utilizing the decaying factor. DHOPM performs the mining of damped high occupancy patterns around this novel upper bound. DHOPM combines DHO-List nodes in a DFS manner while checking the expandability of patterns using this value. Finally, it is determined whether the generated patterns are valid by comparing their damped occupancy with the predefined threshold.

**Lemma 1.** The proposed algorithm extracts novel patterns different from the existing high occupancy pattern mining by considering the arrival time of transactions.

**Proof.** For a given database DB (DB = {T1, …, Tk, Tk+1,…, TL}), assume that there is an arbitrary pattern X and a transaction set T where X occurred in DB, T = {Tx,Ty,…,Tz}, where T ⊆ DB. When the length of X is l, O(X), the occupancy of X, is l/|Tx| + l/|Ty| + …+ l/|Tz|. The novelty of the patterns extracted by DHOPM is proven in the following case: Pattern X is a high occupancy pattern but cannot be a damped high occupancy pattern. To calculate the damped occupancy of X, the decaying factor considering the arrival time of transactions must be considered. In other words, DO(X) = l/|Tx| × f^(TL−Tx) + l/|Ty| × f^(TL−Ty) + …+ l/|Tz| × f^(TL−Tz). The decaying factor constant f is set to a positive real number less than one. Therefore, O(X) > DO(X) always true, since l/|Tx| > l/|Tx| × f^(TL−Tx), l/|Ty| > l/|Ty| × f^(TL−Ty), …, l/|Tz| > l/|Tz| × f^(TL−Tz). Even if O(X) is greater than or equal to minSup, there can be an instance where O(X) > minOcc > DO(X). This case occurs more frequently as the differences between TL and the TIDs of transactions belonging to T increase. This means that a high occupancy pattern that has not occurred recently may not be considered a damped occupancy pattern. ∎

**Definition 6.** (Damped Upper Bound Occupancy). Let T be the set of transactions in which pattern X appears, and the set of lengths of transactions in T be L. In L, transactions with the same length are merged, and all elements are sorted in ascending order. Assume that the size of L is u. Next, the number of transactions whose length is lk is denoted as nk (1 ≤ k ≤ u). The largest TID among transactions with length lk is defined as Tk, and the latest TID among all scanned transactions is expressed as TL (TL ≥ Tk). DUBO(X), the Damped Upper Bound Occupancy of pattern X, is calculated as follows.

DUBO(X, k) = ∑ᵢ₌ₖᵘ nᵢ × lk/li × f^(TL−Tk) (7)

DUBO(X) = max₁≤k≤u DUBO(X, k) (8)

If DUBO(X) is less than minSup, all super patterns of X cannot be damped high occupancy patterns (detailed in Lemma 2). That is, pattern X cannot be set as a prefix pattern during the mining process. Separately, whether X is a qualified pattern is identified by comparing DO with minSup.

### 3.8. Complexity analysis

In this section, the complexity of the proposed algorithm, DHOPM, is analyzed in detail. The algorithm goes through the following steps: Construction, Reconstruction, and Mining. Assume that the costs of each procedure are denoted by T(C), T(R), and T(M), where T(M) represents the cost of generating all patterns that exist in a given database. When a threshold is set to ∂, the complexity of DHOPM is computed by summing the costs of each procedure. Thus, the complexity of DHOPM can be considered as O(T(C) + T(R) + T(M)). However, DHOPM adopts a novel measure relying on a decaying factor f (0 < f < 1) and an occupancy O, called DO, to discover damped high occupancy patterns. Using the measure DO means that, given the same ∂, DHOPM has a lower cost for generating conditional DHO-Lists through the pattern combination process during the mining procedure than T(M). To re-formulate the DHOPM's mining cost based on this principle, let ∂ be the threshold to extract valid patterns, and f be the decaying factor constant for considering the time-decaying properties of the database. Given ∂ and f, C(f, ∂) denotes the number of patterns pruned by predicting unpromising patterns according to f and ∂, i.e., the number of conditional DHO-Lists not constructed. Therefore, the actual cost of DHOPM generating the conditional lists can be expressed as T(M)− C(f, ∂). As a result, the cost for obtaining damped high occupancy patterns using DHOPM is calculated as O(T(C) + T(R) + {T(M) − C(f, ∂)}).

## 5. Discussion and future work

The proposed approach, DHOPM, mines occupancy patterns efficiently in stream environments, considering the flow of time. It assigns higher importance to newer data by multiplying a real number between 0 and 1, called the decaying factor, by the occupancy measure, which makes it possible to discover more interesting patterns than previous methods. It also shows superior performance according to the size of a database and threshold. While the proposed method comes with great benefits, we believe there is still room for improvement. In this section, we would like to discuss this topic in detail and also present some future work regarding the matter. First, the proposed damped occupancy pattern mining approach requires two user-defined values: the minimum support threshold and the decaying factor constant. Accordingly, it can be difficult for users who do not have domain knowledge of the damped occupancy field to define these values efficiently. For this reason, it may be challenging to obtain the desired result by appropriately adjusting these values. This opens up opportunities for experimenting with combinations of thresholds or theoretically developing new metrics. Next, though DHOPM uses an efficient upper bound called DUBO to quickly reduce the search space, studying and developing new pruning techniques can further improve the performance of the algorithm. Experimenting with different types of data structures can also contribute to the optimization of the algorithm. Since DHOPM operates in time-sensitive incremental databases, improving the efficiency of the algorithm for larger datasets is a subject of great interest.

## 6. Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a novel approach and an efficient algorithm for discovering high occupancy patterns considering the arrival time of transactions in an incremental data environment. A new measure called damped occupancy was designed with the damped window-based framework to discover more interesting patterns. The damped occupancy concept effectively considers the importance of transactions according to their arrival times, as well as the frequency and proportions of patterns. Consequently, we extracted damped high occupancy patterns, which are more meaningful than the results of prior high occupancy pattern mining methods. The scheme in this paper includes a new pruning technique called DUBO, which was designed with the damped window model in mind and was suitable for the proposed approach. This technique used in mining efficiency guarantees the anti-monotone property and does not cause any pattern loss. Through elaborate performance evaluation using real and synthetic datasets, we showed that the proposed method is more efficient than the list-based state-of-the-art methods even under the same conditions. However, data occurs infinitely in real-world data stream environments and may require more efficient and feasible processing techniques. Thus, this matter should be considered as important as time-sensitive features. As part of our feature work, we plan on developing new data structures or upper bounds that can more reliably explore high occupancy patterns. Moreover, we will research not only new measures for considering various factors in data but also establish their theoretical foundation.
